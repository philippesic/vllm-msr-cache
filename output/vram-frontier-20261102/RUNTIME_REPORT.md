# Operational record

This follow-up reuses the completed quantized campaign's private candidate, model and public workload. No scoring, kernel, KV ownership, publication, headroom or wait-policy optimization was made for this sweep. The canonical dirty checkout and all old campaign evidence were preserved. Nothing was committed or pushed.

The official Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct AWQ model runs on the actual RTX 2080 Ti with FP16 activations/KV and the qualified Marlin/Triton V2 path. GPU blocks are 16 tokens and 917,504 bytes each. CPU cache is fixed at 4,096 blocks / 3.5 GiB. Reducing the statically allocated GPU KV pool frees real allocator space in both arms. The semantic mechanism may speculatively copy exact cold suffix pages from the same CPU cache before a known pending session returns, then publish ready pages at native demand lookup. Such copies and GPU reservations have costs; memory savings attributable to right-sizing ARC are not a prefetch win.

All runs use 24 natural public agent sessions, at most eight native active requests, three actual-output turns and an EOS-respected 96-token output cap. Later tool observations are external recorded public content rather than execution of this model's commands. Waits are simulated seeded 0–12 second delays; the policy sees the same fixed 750 ms public estimate without the private next input or deadline. This is closed-loop burst-load evidence, not a steady-state endpoint capacity test.

## Strict acceptance and retained failures

The controller checks `summary.proofs`, response success, complete submission identities and descendant cleanup. General `summary.valid` alone is insufficient. A genuine old failed-proof raw is a CPU negative control. Time-capped workloads are censored; completed responses still need strict proofs, and unfinished requests are never manufactured as successful terminal rows.

The first 1,024-block candidate attempt completed 72 responses but passed only 69 strict proofs. The screen stopped. The native offload scheduler returns `(None, False)` before any manager lookup while a live request owns pending transfer jobs. An old observer incorrectly finished an empty lookup episode around this no-query path. One bounded common observer-only correction was qualified with 23 CPU controls, then 72 strict real-model requests, eight preemptions and three directly observed same-request pending-job deferrals with zero actual manager queries. The performance query counter is disabled and its observed count is null. An earlier zero-request import-plumbing failure is retained. The strict validator is unchanged. See `qualification/lookup-boundary-diagnosis.json`, `lookup_deferral_observer.py` and `runs/qualify-native-defer1024-v2/qualification-summary.json`.

The fresh 1,280-block candidate attempt completed 72 responses but passed only 71 strict proofs. A read-only trace identified the validator's aggregate unique physical-destination guard: 25 pages recur across two demand-load jobs whose worker intervals are separated by approximately 3.24 seconds. Nonoverlap does not prove complete ownership/key/fence correctness. No second observer/guard repair was made and the point remains invalid. See `qualification/physical-gap1280-cause.json` and `qualification/physical-gap1280-validator-trace.json`. The five independent missing points were resumed without source, policy or observer changes under `CONTINUATION_PLAN.json`; point acceptance was never waived. The final ARC 1,536 point also failed the same exact guard: 169 job-qualified destinations project to 168 physical pages, with one page appearing in two nonoverlapping demand jobs. It remains invalid. See `qualification/physical-gap1536-arc-cause.json`.

The valid original 2,048-block pair uses the older common observer. Every other fresh pair uses the qualified pending-transfer observer. This per-point provenance is explicit in the results; old failed attempts are not silently replaced by passes. Existing dedicated KV byte audits and native-preemption qualification are linked from `PREFLIGHT.json`; synchronous byte audits are excluded from throughput measurements.

## Interpretation limits

Generated outputs, subsequent histories and output lengths may differ across arms. Comparison inventories preserve those differences; descriptive useful-token throughput is not the original exact-work 1% claim. Numerical scoring and native adoption are reported separately, including no-op high-memory controls. This campaign does not establish scoring-quality equivalence or general production safety. All final operating recommendations require the parent's scientific disposition.

Final measured coverage, duration, cleanup and reproduction receipts are recorded in the derived result files, `STORAGE.json`, `FINAL_PINS.json` and `REPRODUCE.md` after the sweep closes.

## Measured disposition

All sixteen intended points ran: fourteen complete valid points / 1,008 accepted strict responses, two invalid holes and no time-capped points. The old failed 1,024 attempt remains separate. There is no low-memory prefetch advantage in this screen. At 768 blocks ARC delivered 48.02 output tokens/s with 49.15 s p95 first-token latency; the candidate delivered 41.36 tokens/s with 63.57 s latency despite 73 numerical scoring calls and one native adoption. At 1,024 blocks the corresponding rates were 56.81 versus 54.94 tokens/s. The 1,280 candidate and 1,536 ARC are correctness-ineligible; their diagnostics cannot establish a comparative frontier.

The predeclared 4,096-block ARC reference delivered 79.89 tokens/s. At 2,048 blocks ARC delivered 79.77 and the candidate 79.01, with p95 first-token latencies 19.84 and 20.39 s. Both use 1.75 GiB KV pools; measured peak CUDA allocations were 7.207 and 7.215 GiB. Relative to ARC's 4,096-block allocation, reducing ARC to 2,048 saves 1.75 GiB of real CUDA allocation while retaining 99.85% descriptive throughput. This is ARC right-sizing. It is not memory uniquely freed by semantic prefetch. The candidate has 27 numerical scoring calls, five native adoptions, one preemption and about 1.15 GiB completed speculative H2D traffic, of which about 0.58 GiB was consumed by its named demand. Its 712-block speculative reservation peak and 447-block request-owned peak are within the configured pool and can reduce active capacity; they are not extra free VRAM.

Largest-budget ARC is not the fastest observed ARC: the 3,072-block point delivered 82.04 tokens/s, approximately 2.7% faster. `THRESHOLDS.json/CSV` retain the predeclared largest-budget normalization and separately show the posthoc highest-observed sensitivity. With throughput retention / +5% p95 TTFT, primary 99% minima are ARC 2,048 and candidate 2,560; primary 97/95% minima are both 2,048. Secondary 99% minima are ARC 2,560 and candidate 3,072; secondary 97% minima are ARC 2,048 and candidate 2,560; secondary 95% minima are both 2,048. These single-screen thresholds depend on the reference and are not fitted optima. Every accepted point misses the prior absolute 15 s p95 TTFT criterion.

The predeclared fallback selects the 1,536→2,048 boundary, which is partly invalid and establishes no optimum. The parent's final disposition authorizes no further GPU confirmation because no advantage emerged and the earlier campaign already provides three paired 2,048-block external repetitions. Those earlier candidate/small-ARC throughput ratios are 1.0228, 0.9752 and 1.0110 (geometric mean 1.0028), with unequal generated histories. `EXTERNAL_REPLICATION.json` records the exact reused source/math/model/corpus controls and common observer/ledger/cap differences. These data are not pooled and are not a fresh-revision equivalence claim. High-memory zero-adoption points are controls; only the 4,096 pair was exact-work eligible, with no scoring. A 1,536-block candidate retaining at least 99% of ARC 2,048 and +5% TTFT would save another 448 MiB of pool; current 70.43 tokens/s does not achieve that aspiration.

Measured run lifecycles sum to 41.26 minutes for the sixteen screen points, including 27.11 minutes of actual traffic and 11.47 minutes startup/warmup. The old failed attempt adds 3.00 minutes; three qualification/import attempts add 4.10 minutes. First qualification to final measurement spans 73.11 minutes, with 24.75 minutes outside per-run lifecycles for diagnosis/repair/planning, CPU metrics/checks and process gaps. That residual is not a pure repair stopwatch. Plotting/report/cleanup time is additional. `DURATION.json` retains each timestamp. Accepted points have small recorded host paging (swap-in maximum 868,352 bytes; process major faults 1–8), not zero paging. The invalid candidate 1,280 point has 4,325,376 swap-in bytes. Full CPU/RSS/allocator/NVML/transfer/latency data remain in `SCREEN_RESULTS.csv` and raw summaries.

The practical experimental target supported here is approximately the 2,048-block region, with the explicit finite-load/SLO/provenance limitations above. The sole candidate 1,536 nondominated label is an artifact of a missing valid ARC point at that budget, not evidence of semantic superiority. No production deployment, scoring-quality equivalence, steady-state capacity or exact-work 99% claim is accepted by this operational report.
